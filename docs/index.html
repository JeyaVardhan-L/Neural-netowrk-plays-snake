A neural network that teaches itself Snake

I built this to stop treating neural networks as a black box. There is no PyTorch and no TensorFlow here. The network is two matrix multiplications and a ReLU, the backpropagation is six lines I wrote by hand, and the learning signal is three numbers: +10 for food, −10 for a crash, 0 for everything else.

The snake sees 11 simple inputs, like danger ahead or food to the left, and picks one of three moves: straight, right or left. At first it plays at random and dies in a few steps. A few hundred games later, about 12 seconds of training on my laptop, it averages 26 apples a game and chases food while dodging its own tail.

This guide is the journal I kept while building it. Every chapter is one milestone, with the code that did it, a figure you can play with, the numbers I actually measured, and the interview question I would expect about it. It is honest about what did not matter and where the agent still fails. If you follow it in order you can rebuild the project from an empty folder.

  3. 03Eleven numbersThe network never sees the board. It sees eleven 0s and 1s, and it answers with a move relative to its own head. Both choices shape everything that follows.the state
  4. 04One neuronA neuron multiplies, adds and then clips at zero. The clipping looks like a detail; without it, this snake provably cannot learn to play.neurons, ReLU
  5. 05The forward passSixty-four neurons at once is a matrix multiply. The whole network is three lines of NumPy, and the shapes tell you almost everything.matrices
  6. 06Loss and backprop by handBackpropagation is the chain rule, applied to each line of the forward pass from the bottom up. Six lines of NumPy, and a test that proves they are right.gradients
  7. 07Gradient descent and AdamA gradient says which way is downhill. How far to step is a separate question, and Adam answers it differently for every weight.optimisers
  8. 08Rewards and Q-learningThere is no dataset of correct moves. The target the network learns from is built from the reward it just got plus its own guess about what comes next.Bellman
  9. 09Explore, then exploitAn agent that always takes its best-known move never finds out whether another move is better. Epsilon-greedy is the simplest fix, and here it mattered less than I expected.epsilon-greedy
  10. 10Replay memory and the target networkThe two ideas that turned Q-learning into Deep Q-Networks. I built both, then switched them off to see what they were worth.DQN

  13. 13Looking inside: it learned the ruleI wrote down the obvious strategy as five lines of Python and compared. The network had rediscovered it, and agrees with it on 94% of the moves it plays.interpretation
